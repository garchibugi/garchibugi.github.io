## Work in progress

**Railways, Identity and Cultural Change in Post-Unification Italy**  
with Giacomo Gattorno

<details style="margin-top: -1em; margin-bottom: 2em;">
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<p style="margin-top: 0.5em;">This paper investigates whether railroad expansion contributed to nation-building and cultural integration in post-unification Italy between 1861 and 1913. We construct a new municipality-year dataset from digitized civil birth registers and military conscription lists, using first names as markers of identity and local cultural practices. We distinguish between the adoption of names explicitly associated with the Italian nation, and broader changes in naming behavior, including the diffusion of locally rare names and the decline of geographically distinctive naming patterns. Our baseline analysis exploits the staggered opening of railway stations across municipalities. To address the endogenous placement of railroads, we complement these estimates with an instrumental-variable strategy based on least-cost paths. Holding historically targeted cities fixed, we construct counterfactual routes determined by the geographic cost of building on the intervening terrain and create fictitious stations along these routes to isolate plausibly exogenous variation in railway access. We find that municipalities gaining a station experienced a significant increase in the use of patriotic and royal names, with the instrumental-variable estimates reinforcing the baseline results. Railroad access is also associated with greater name novelty and, in the baseline specifications, with a weakening of province-specific naming patterns. Overall, the findings suggest that transport infrastructure affected not only economic connectivity, but also the formation of national identity and the permeability of local cultural conventions.</p>
